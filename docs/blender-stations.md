# Blender station asset specification — draft v1

Model three fictional heavy-metro stations with the same 150 m length and different widths. These are gameplay asset dimensions, not a real engineering design. Start with one rough grey-box model to check scale and sockets before detailing all three.

## Shared dimensions and datum

| Asset | Outer length | Outer width | Platform arrangement | Platform dimensions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `station_narrow` | 150 m | 18 m | Central island | 140 × 6.6 m, centred at Y=0 |
| `station_medium` | 150 m | 24 m | Two side platforms | 140 × 4.8 m each, centred at Y=±9.1 m |
| `station_wide` | 150 m | 30 m | Two side platforms | 140 × 7.8 m each, centred at Y=±10.6 m |

- Blender units: Metric, Unit Scale 1.0; one model unit is one metre.
- X runs along the station, Y across it, Z up. Origin `(0, 0, 0)` is the midpoint of the station at the **top-of-rail datum**, not the floor or roof.
- Station endpoints are X=−75 and X=+75. Playable platform endpoints are X=−70 and X=+70. Outer width includes the walls; keep walls within the envelope.
- Both track centrelines are at Y=−5 and Y=+5, Z=0 for every variant. Rail gauge for these assets is 1.435 m, measured between the inside faces of the rails. The temporary procedural rails only approximate this detail.
- Platform walking surface Z=+0.7 m; track-bed surface Z=−0.6 m. These match the current prototype. Floor slab can extend down to Z=−1.2 m.
- Minimum modelling envelope above rail: roof underside at Z=+5.0 m, outer roof up to +5.5 m. Maintain a clear train envelope around each track: centre Y=±5, width 3.4 m, height 4.2 m from Z=0. Platform edges may meet its lateral boundary (Y=±3.3 m for the island, Y=±6.7 m for side platforms); columns must remain outside it. A reference 3.2 m train body leaves a 0.1 m horizontal platform gap. This is an asset integration constraint, not a certified train gauge.
- A narrow island station needs stairs/columns inside the island; side-platform variants put them on the outer platforms. Do not stretch one finished station mesh to create the other variants.

## Object hierarchy

Use one root Empty named exactly as the asset. Keep these child objects distinct:

- `station_body`: walls and track-bed floor, visible in surface and underground representations as appropriate.
- `platforms`: platform slabs, edges and walking surfaces.
- `columns`: structural supports.
- `roof`: separate mesh, hidden for the underground cutaway.
- `details`: signs, benches and minor details; separate reusable objects where useful.
- `sockets`: an Empty containing named connector Empties.

Rails and sleepers are generated by the game. Use optional hidden reference rails in Blender to check clearances, but exclude them from the final GLB. Do not model long approach tunnels: the engine generates them from the trace curve and joins their two tracks to the station sockets.

## Connector Empties

All coordinates below are in Blender space relative to the station root:

| Name | X | Y | Z | Outward direction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `track_west_south` | −75 | −5 | 0 | −X |
| `track_west_north` | −75 | +5 | 0 | −X |
| `track_east_south` | +75 | −5 | 0 | +X |
| `track_east_north` | +75 | +5 | 0 | +X |
| `bore_west` | −75 | 0 | 0 | −X |
| `bore_east` | +75 | 0 | 0 | +X |

The socket's local +X points outward and local +Z points up: use Z rotation 180° on west sockets and 0° on east sockets. The bore socket is a **rail-level origin for the cross-section**, not the geometric centre of a circular tunnel. Its opening profile depends on the station width; the procedural transition adapts to it.

Add accessible attachment points under `sockets` for the mezzanine/entrances. Name them `access_01`, `access_02`, etc. Place them on a platform walking surface, Z=+0.7. For the island variant put them on the island; for side variants provide attachments on both platforms. Full pedestrian routing is future work.

## Entrance asset, separate from the station

Create one reusable `entrance_stairs` asset. Its origin is the centre of the surface footprint at ground Z=0. Use X along its staircase, Y across, Z up. Approximate footprint 10 × 5 m; canopy top around +3.4 m. The street-facing stair opening faces **−X**, stairs descend toward +X. Keep the canopy, posts, stairs and signs under the same root so the engine rotates them together. Surface entrance placement is independent of station depth; the future access corridor connects it to an `access_*` socket.

Do not bake four entrances or 100 m access shafts into the station asset. The game places/rotates instances and constructs access geometry separately. The current demo uses two entrances facing west and two east.

## Export

Deliver the editable `.blend` and one `.glb` per asset: `station_narrow.glb`, `station_medium.glb`, `station_wide.glb`, `entrance_stairs.glb`.

1. Apply mesh rotation and scale; mesh scales should be `(1,1,1)`. Preserve the root origin, child positions and deliberate socket rotations. Applying mesh rotation is not an instruction to reset socket headings.
2. Export glTF 2.0 as GLB, Selected Objects, including root and socket Empties. Leave standard **+Y Up** conversion enabled. Model in Blender Z-up; do not manually rotate the whole station before export.
3. Use Principled BSDF materials with base colour, roughness and optional image textures. Avoid Blender-only procedural shaders in deliverables; bake needed textures. Start with simple opaque materials.
4. Use metre-scale UVs where repeating surfaces need them. Recalculate outward normals; roof must be separable rather than relying on every material being double-sided.
5. Target about 30,000–60,000 triangles per station and under 5,000 per entrance for the first detailed version. These are provisional performance budgets. Prefer reusable details and textures of 1K–2K; verify with the game before increasing complexity.
6. Re-import the GLB into an empty Blender scene and verify 150 m length, width, rail datum and socket positions. Include an optional screenshot with a 1 m cube to make scale review easy.

Standard glTF export converts Blender `(X,Y,Z)` to glTF `(X,Z,−Y)`. The future importer will convert the loaded root back to the engine's local Z-up basis exactly once; connectors and geometry must use the same conversion. Exported rail sockets therefore become `(X,0,−Y)` in the GLB. Do not compensate twice.

## How curved tunnels will work

Station geometry stays rigid. The player supplies a route centreline with elevations. Code samples a smooth 3D curve, transports a consistent cross-section frame along it, builds the tunnel shell, offsets two track paths and instances sleepers along distance. End position, tangent and elevation must match a station connector before joining. Different station widths use a transition section; the station mesh is not bent to fit a curve.

This specification does not add a production curved-route builder or a GLB loader. Those follow after a grey-box asset proves this contract. Current procedural demo approaches are a visual prototype and are not train-speed or curvature validation.
